Here's something I can't wrap my head around: The engineers and researchers at the very front of AI, the people actually building these models, are openly saying there's a significant chance it destroys humanity. This week Jacob Coxon, a researcher who worked at both OpenAI and Anthropic, [resigned in a thread on X](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476196791709843) saying the people building AI believe it could kill us all by the end of the decade. Anthropic's alignment science lead, Evan Hubinger, [replied that Coxon was right](https://x.com/evanhub/status/2097497037956891126) and put the odds above 10% within the next decade. And yet investors keep pouring record amounts of money into the same companies.

Why? If the builders themselves are scared, what are investors seeing that makes the bet worth it?

The best answer I've found is a 350-year-old argument about God by Blaise Pascal which he wrote in his book <i>Pensées</i>

## Pascal's wager, briefly

Blaise Pascal argued it's rational to believe in God even without proof, because the payoffs are lopsided:

| | God exists | God doesn't exist |
|---|---|---|
| **Believe** | Infinite gain (heaven) | Small loss (some pleasures skipped) |
| **Don't believe** | Infinite loss (hell) | Small gain (those pleasures kept) |

You don't need to know the odds. One column is so extreme it decides the bet for you. More on Pascal's wager [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal%27s_wager)

## The investor's version

Swap God for the AI outcome, and belief for a check:

| | AI goes well | AI destroys humanity (~10% chance) |
|---|---|---|
| **Invest** | Own the most valuable companies ever built | $0 |
| **Don't invest** | Miss it | $0 |

Same shape, one twist. In the doom column, everyone ends at zero whether they invested or not. There's no portfolio, no payout, nobody left to collect. A column that's identical for every choice can't affect the choice, so it drops out. All that's left is the upside, and there investing always wins.

That's the answer. Investors aren't ignoring the risk. The math makes it irrelevant to them.

## Where the parallel breaks

Pascal's wager has one hidden assumption: your bet doesn't change whether God exists.

Investors treat AI risk the same way, as a fixed number outside their control. Their reasoning is that someone will build it anyway, so they might as well own a piece. But AI isn't God... Yes it's NOT! The odds of it going wrong depend on how fast and how carelessly it gets built, and investor money is what funds that speed. Each investor thinks they're just placing a bet on the outcome. Together, they're shaping it.

## The takeaway

Pascal's logic works for one person facing a reality they can't change. AI investment is thousands of people betting on a reality they're collectively creating. Because the worst case zeroes out everyone equally, no individual bet will ever price that risk in. That's the real reason the money keeps flowing, and it's exactly why this can't be left to the market alone.
